Experimental single-owner runs. Where the deployment can host a run's owner (a World that implements `invoke`, the current deployment, a static workflow), `start()` marks the run with the reserved attribute `$experimentalSingleOwner` (`'{}'` routes it by its own ID; a caller may pass `{"vercelAffinity": ...}`). Every input — the start, hook resumes, wakes, step results and cancellation — is invoked on one owner, which keeps the workflow resident, validates transitions locally and writes the run's history through one serialized writer. The owner runs up to three step bodies itself and delivers the rest directly to other invocations; their results return through `invoke`. A queued wake for a run that has ended is acknowledged rather than redelivered. The owner's same-deployment requests (invokes, step delivery and results) run outside the context of whichever request woke it, so the platform's `x-vercel-id` hop chain stays at a constant depth instead of growing with every overflow round trip and tripping loop detection (508); a 508 refusal is retried as an undelivered step. A step running in the owner, or dispatched over a request still in flight, has no time limit; only attempts the owner cannot observe (inherited from a previous owner, or a dispatch that ended without a known outcome) are recovered, after 15 minutes. Up to 2000 steps of a run may run at once, and a worker retries its result delivery from 1 s plus up to 1 s of jitter, doubling to 120 s. The World contract gains `queue.invoke` and an optional single-writer event session (`events.createWriteSession`).
