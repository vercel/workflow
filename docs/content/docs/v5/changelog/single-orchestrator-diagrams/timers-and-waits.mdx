# Timers and waits

<Callout type="warn">
This page is temporary review material for the pull request that introduces spec version 9, and is removed before merge. The written review is [Review: single orchestrator](/docs/changelog/single-orchestrator-review).
</Callout>

On spec 9, the orchestrator writes both events of a sleep in-band: `wait_created` when the workflow reaches `sleep()`, and `wait_completed` at the top of a pass once the wait is due (`dueWaits`, `packages/core/src/runtime/spec9/delivery.ts:2452`). Between the two, a timer message wakes the run. A timer is a delayed orchestrator message carrying `waitContinuation`, the wait it is for. Spec 9 timers carry no idempotency key, except the near-due rule's re-arms ("The near-due re-arm key" below).

The pieces:

- `waitTimerMessage` (`spec9/wait-timer.ts`) builds the message: the delay from `getWaitContinuationDispatch` (at most `WAIT_CONTINUATION_MAX_DELAY_SECONDS`, 82800 seconds), and `resumeAtMs` on every hop but the last.
- `sendWaitTimer` (`spec9/delivery.ts:3822`) sends one timer through `wakeSelf`, which stamps the run's spec version.
- `armTimers` (`spec9/delivery.ts:3802`) sends the timers this delivery owns, once per wait per delivery.
- `ownedWaitChains`, `schedulesWaitTimer` and `waitTimersToArm` (`spec9/creator-rules.ts`) decide which timers those are.

## Who arms a timer

Each `wait_created` records `creatorMessageId`, the queue message of the delivery that wrote it. All waits written by one message form a chain. A delivery owns, and arms the earliest open wait of:

- its own message's chain, including waits it is about to write;
- the chain of every wait whose `wait_completed` it committed, whatever message it is;
- on a redelivery of a timer message, that timer's chain;
- and the wait of a timer delivered before its wait is due (`timerFor`), which re-arms what is left.

Two more rules cover the gaps chain ownership left:

- A delivery that is about to commit the `wait_completed` of a wait it does not own through `timerFor` first sends the timer of the chain's next open wait (`chainTimersBeforeCompletion`, `3ba56bbd43`). A failure after the commit then leaves the chain armed; a failed send commits nothing, and the redelivery decides again.
- A suspending delivery that may hold a coalesced timer (any delivery but its message's first) arms every open wait due within `max(5s, WORKFLOW_NEAR_ELAPSED_WAIT_THRESHOLD_SECONDS)`, whatever its chain (`getNearDueWaitWindowSeconds`, `66b355d28c`), so a final timer coalesced slightly before its wait is due, into a delivery that does not own it, is not lost. Each such re-arm carries an idempotency key, so the cost stays linear in the number of waits ("The near-due re-arm key" below).

A recovery delivery (world-local after a restart, `recovery: true` on the message) owns every chain and arms the earliest open wait of each: the timers died with the queue.

Every other delivery leaves a chain alone, so timers do not pile up across suspensions. A late or duplicate timer finds its wait completed and arms nothing (`packages/core/src/runtime/spec9/creator-rules.ts:112`).

```mermaid
flowchart TD
    W["Open wait W"] --> T{"This delivery is W's own timer?"}
    T -->|"yes"| ARM["May arm W"]
    T -->|"no"| N{"W in the log yet?"}
    N -->|"no, about to write it"| ARM
    N -->|"yes"| C{"W's creatorMessageId in owned chains?"}
    C -->|"yes"| ARM
    C -->|"no"| SKIP["Leave W alone"]
    ARM --> E{"Earliest open wait of its chain, or the timer's own wait?"}
    E -->|"yes"| SEND["sendWaitTimer"]
    E -->|"no"| WAITN["Armed later by whoever completes the earlier wait"]
```

The delivery that completes a wait arms its chain's next timer right after the completion, not at suspend (`armTimers(log.events, 'other-chains')`, `packages/core/src/runtime/spec9/delivery.ts:2477`). The delivery may end in a handoff first, and the next delivery does not know which waits this one completed.

## Two sleeps in one `Promise.all`

{/* @skip-typecheck - illustrative workflow */}
```typescript
export async function reminders() {
  "use workflow";
  await Promise.all([sleep("1h"), sleep("2h")]);
}
```

Both waits are created by message `m1`, so they form one chain, and only the earliest gets a timer.

```mermaid
sequenceDiagram
    participant Q as Queue
    participant D1 as Delivery of m1
    participant D2 as Delivery of timer T1
    participant D3 as Delivery of timer T2
    participant L as Event log
    Q->>D1: deliver m1
    D1->>L: wait_created w1 1h, w2 2h, creator m1
    D1->>Q: timer T1 for w1, delay 1h, keyed
    Note over D1: suspend, chain m1 has one timer
    Q->>D2: deliver T1 at 1h
    D2->>L: wait_completed w1
    Note over D2: completed w1, so owns chain m1
    D2->>Q: timer T2 for w2, delay about 1h
    Q->>D3: deliver T2 at 2h
    D3->>L: wait_completed w2
    Note over D3: Promise.all resolves, run continues
```

An earlier rule let only the wait's own timer delivery arm the next one. A timer coalesced into another delivery then lost the second sleep's timer. See [Edge cases](/docs/changelog/single-orchestrator-diagrams/edge-cases).

## Long sleeps are reached in hops

A wait more than 82800 seconds (23 hours) away is reached in hops. Every hop but the last carries the wait's deadline (`waitContinuation.resumeAtMs`), which `isIntermediateWaitHop` reads. An intermediate hop has no concurrency key on world-vercel and no per-run gate on world-local and world-postgres. Its delivery reads only the run's status, writes nothing, sends the next hop, and acknowledges (`packages/core/src/runtime/spec9/delivery.ts:875`). The last hop is an ordinary keyed timer.

```mermaid
flowchart TD
    S["seconds until resumeAt"] --> D{"More than 82800?"}
    D -->|"yes"| I["Intermediate hop, delay 82800, resumeAtMs set, unkeyed"]
    D -->|"no"| F["Final hop, delay = seconds, no resumeAtMs, keyed"]
    I --> R["Hop delivery: runs.get, status only"]
    R --> X{"Run terminal or missing?"}
    X -->|"yes"| STOP["Acknowledge, chain stops"]
    X -->|"no"| S
    F --> O["Orchestrator delivery: wait due, wait_completed"]
```

A 3-day sleep (259200 seconds):

```mermaid
sequenceDiagram
    participant D as Orchestrator delivery
    participant Q as Queue
    participant H as Hop delivery
    participant O as Later orchestrator delivery
    D->>Q: hop 1, delay 82800, resumeAtMs, no key
    Q->>H: hop 1 at 23h
    H->>H: runs.get, run still sleeping
    H->>Q: hop 2, delay 82800, resumeAtMs, no key
    Q->>H: hop 2 at 46h
    H->>Q: hop 3, delay 82800, resumeAtMs, no key
    Q->>H: hop 3 at 69h
    H->>Q: final hop, delay 10800, keyed
    Q->>O: final hop at 72h
    O->>O: load log, wait due, write wait_completed
```

An intermediate hop is never coalesced. A coalesced hop is never delivered, and the holder's re-run would find the wait not yet due and send no next hop (`packages/world/src/queue.ts:635`).

## A timer coalesced into a running delivery

A final timer is keyed. If it comes due while another delivery of the run holds the key (a hook wake, a step wake), Vercel Queues coalesces it. The timer's payload is lost, and that is harmless: the holder's re-run reads the log, finds the wait due, completes it, and, having completed it, owns its chain.

```mermaid
sequenceDiagram
    participant C as resumeHook caller
    participant Q as Vercel Queues
    participant O as Hook wake delivery
    participant L as Event log
    Note over L: open waits w1 due in a moment and w2 due in 1h, chain m1
    C->>L: hook_received
    C->>Q: wake, keyed
    Q->>O: deliver hook wake
    O->>L: load, replay, act on the hook
    Q-->>Q: timer T1 for w1 comes due, key held, coalesced
    O->>Q: acknowledge
    Q-->>O: refused, 1 coalesced
    Note over O: re-run, reads the log
    O->>L: wait_completed w1
    O->>Q: timer T2 for w2, armed by the completing delivery
    O->>Q: acknowledge, If-Coalesced 1
    Note over O: a holder still lingering completes w1 in a pass of its own, and the re-run finds nothing new
```

If the key was released before a keyed timer came due, the review states that Vercel Queues compares the timer's due time with the release and delivers it normally. That is queue server behavior and is not verified here.

## The near-due re-arm key

The near-due rule's re-arms are the only spec 9 timers with an idempotency key: `wait:<correlationId>:<resumeAtMs>:<second>`, where the second is `floor(nowMs / 1000)` on the sender's clock (`nearDueTimerIdempotencyKey`, `packages/core/src/runtime/spec9/wait-timer.ts`). It identifies one re-arm of one wait, for one deadline, within one second.

**Why the rule needed one.** Without a key, every suspending delivery sent a timer for every open wait due within the window, and nothing deduplicated them across deliveries. A fan-out of `step(i); sleep('3s')` whose outcomes each woke the run in its own delivery (no coalescing and no live feed: world-local, world-postgres, world-vercel without the push transport) sent `n(n + 1) / 2` timers instead of `n`. On QuickJS a wake that brought nothing new re-armed too.

**Who re-arms now.** Only a delivery that may hold a coalesced timer: any delivery but its message's first (`mayHoldCoalescedTimer`, `packages/core/src/runtime/spec9/delivery.ts`; `armsNearDueWaits` in `quickjs-entrypoint.ts`). A coalesced message never reaches the first run of the delivery that absorbs it: the holder runs again, and the re-run reports the next `deliveryCount`. A process also sends each key once (`sendNearDueTimerOnce`, the 10,000 newest keys), so a busy run's re-runs do not each pay a queue round trip per near-due wait.

**Which sends carry it.** Only the near-due re-arm. These stay unkeyed:

- the timer a wait's own chain arms (at creation, or after the chain's previous wait completes). A re-arm is sent by the delivery the queue may have coalesced that timer into, while that timer is still being delivered, so sharing its key would drop the re-arm against the very timer it covers;
- the chain timers sent before a `wait_completed` commits (`3ba56bbd43`), and a timer delivery's own re-arm, each sent once by the delivery that owns the chain;
- intermediate hops: never coalesced, so never re-armed by this rule, and a key spent by an early hop would drop the next one.

**How each World deduplicates it.**

| World | A second send under the key |
|---|---|
| world-vercel | Dropped for `min(retention, 24h)` after the first send, acknowledged or not |
| world-local | Dropped while the first message lives (held, delayed or being delivered) |
| world-postgres | Dropped while a Graphile job holds the key (`unsafe_dedupe`: waiting, locked or in backoff); each worker also skips a key it completed recently |

**Why the second.** A re-arm is sent with a delay of at least a second, since its wait is not yet due on the sender's clock. If it is itself delivered early and coalesced into another non-owner, that delivery re-arms in a later second of its clock, under a fresh key. A key of the wait alone would be spent by the first re-arm (for a day on world-vercel), and the next one dropped. A sender and receiver whose clocks differ by more than the re-arm's delay can still share a second; spec 8's near-elapsed keys have the same second bucket and the same limit.

**Why `resumeAt`.** Two deadlines must never share a key. Today they cannot: `resumeAt` is fixed by `wait_created`, `run.wakeUp()` completes a wait (`wait_completed`) instead of moving it, and every `sleep()` mints a fresh correlation id (`wait_${ctx.generateUlid()}`, `packages/core/src/workflow/sleep.ts`). The deadline in the key guards against a later change to either.

**Alternatives considered.** The gate alone (on world-vercel every coalesced re-run of a busy run would still send a timer per near-due wait); the key alone (every first delivery of every wake would send a re-arm the queue then drops); deduplication within one delivery (the quadratic case); no re-arm (the coalesced-early hole comes back); and the same key on the chain's own timer (drops the re-arm, above).

**Cost**, measured on `AppendOnlyWorld.runOnClock`, which honours delays and keys (`packages/core/src/runtime/spec9/near-due-timers.runtime.test.ts`; timers sent / timers delivered / all deliveries):

| Fan-out of `step(i); sleep('3s')` | Before | After |
|---|---|---|
| 20 wide, one outcome per delivery, both engines | 210 / 210 / 251 | 20 / 20 / 61 |
| 40 wide, the same | 820 / 820 / 901 | 40 / 40 / 121 |
| 40 wide, every step done before the first wake, QuickJS | 1600 / 1600 / 1681 | 1 / 1 / 82 |
| 40 wide, the same, node | 40 / 40 / 121 | 1 / 1 / 82 |
| 40 wide, every delivery a coalesced re-run, frozen clock | | 40 / 40 / 121 |
| 20 and 40 wide, coalesced re-runs a quarter second apart | | node 80 and 120, QuickJS 38 and 36 |

## Timers inside a live delivery

A delivery that is still running inline steps, or lingering on a live feed, completes a due wait itself. `waitForProgress` sleeps at most until the earliest open wait's deadline (`nextTimerAt`, `packages/core/src/runtime/spec9/delivery.ts:1697`), and the next pass writes `wait_completed`. The timer message also goes out before the delivery waits (`armTimers` in `awaitInlineProgress`, `packages/core/src/runtime/spec9/delivery.ts:3533`), so the wait is covered if the delivery ends first. When it comes due while the delivery still holds the key, it is coalesced and costs a re-run that finds nothing to do.

A wait due within the invocation's inline window plus `OPEN_WAIT_CLOCK_SKEW_MS` (30 seconds) makes the boundary path-changing, so the orchestrator does not run ahead past it (`waitDue`, `packages/core/src/runtime/spec9/delivery.ts:2820`). See [Run-ahead and turbo](/docs/changelog/single-orchestrator-diagrams/run-ahead-and-turbo).

## `run.wakeUp()`

On a spec 9 run, `wakeUpRun` does not write `wait_completed`. It sends one orchestrator message with `completeWaits`, the waits to complete now, and returns `stoppedCount` as the number of waits it asked for (`packages/core/src/runtime/runs.ts:476`). The orchestrator completes the named waits on its first pass, whatever their `resumeAt` (`dueWaits` with `wakeUpWaits`, `packages/core/src/runtime/spec9/log-state.ts:210`).

The message is an ordinary keyed orchestrator message, so it is best-effort:

```mermaid
sequenceDiagram
    participant U as wakeUp caller
    participant Q as Queue
    participant O as Orchestrator delivery
    participant L as Event log
    U->>Q: message with completeWaits w1, keyed
    U-->>U: stoppedCount 1
    alt run suspended, nothing holds the key
        Q->>O: deliver the message
        O->>L: wait_completed w1, in-band
        Note over O: sleep ends now
    else world-vercel, an orchestrator delivery holds the key
        Q-->>Q: coalesced into the holder
        O->>L: re-run reads only the log
        Note over O: completeWaits lost, w1 runs its full length
    else world-local or world-postgres, a delivery is running
        Q->>Q: per-run gate holds the message
        Q->>O: delivered after the running delivery
        O->>L: wait_completed w1
    end
```

On world-local the running delivery does not linger past its work, because world-local has no `events.subscribe`, so `@workflow/vitest`'s `waitForSleep()` and `wakeUp()` keep working.

## Why `wait_completed` is in-band only

An out-of-band `wait_completed` could commit below events the orchestrator already acted on while running ahead, and a later replay would see them in a different order. The world-vercel backend refuses an out-of-band `wait_completed` on a spec 9 run with 409 `wait-completion-in-band-only`, and the run-ahead hazard check treats any `wait_completed` from another writer as a hazard (`packages/core/src/runtime/spec9/run-ahead.ts:64`).

{/* @skip-typecheck - illustrative workflow */}
```typescript
export async function digest(userId: string) {
  "use workflow";
  const first = await fetchUpdates(userId); // run-ahead: outcome reaches the workflow before it commits
  const timeout = sleep("1h");
  return await Promise.race([timeout, nextUpdate(userId, first)]);
}
```

If a `wait_completed` committed below `fetchUpdates`'s outcome, the live run saw the step first and a replay would see the wait first.

## Edge cases

- A delivery that commits `wait_completed` and dies before its next send no longer leaves that chain without a timer: the next timer goes out before the commit. Tests in `spec9/wait-chains.runtime.test.ts`, both engines.
- `VERCEL_QUEUE_MAX_DELAY_SECONDS` below 82800 clamps a final hop so it arrives early. Delivered on its own it re-arms its wait; coalesced, it is re-armed by the near-due rule only when it arrives within that window of its wait, so a clamp far below the hop length can still lose it. Not covered by a test.
- A world-local restart loses every queued timer; the recovery delivery re-arms every chain (`world-local-restart-recovery.test.ts`).
- A timer delivered slightly early (clock skew) is its own wait's `timerFor` and re-arms the rest (`packages/core/src/runtime/spec9/creator-rules.ts:161`).
- A timer delivery skips the no-op fast path, because an early one must still arm the next hop (`packages/core/src/runtime/spec9/delivery.ts:978`).
- A delivery that owns waits of two chains arms the earliest of each chain, not the earliest of all (`packages/core/src/runtime/spec9/creator-rules.ts:176`).
- An intermediate hop for a run that ended stops the chain without writing anything (`packages/core/src/runtime/spec9/delivery.ts:885`).
- Spec 9 timers have no idempotency key, except near-due re-arms, so a duplicate timer is possible and costs one delivery that finds its wait completed (`waitTimerMessage`, `packages/core/src/runtime/spec9/wait-timer.ts`).
- A near-due re-arm that is itself delivered early and coalesced into a non-owner is re-armed under a fresh key in a later second; clocks that differ by more than its delay can make the keys collide. Not covered by a test.
- `run.wakeUp()` is lost on world-vercel while an orchestrator delivery holds the key, and `stoppedCount` still counts the waits (`packages/core/src/runtime/runs.ts:468`).
- The review states that the Vercel dashboard's wake-up action calls a pinned `@workflow/core` that writes `wait_completed` out-of-band, which the backend refuses on spec 9 runs; not verified here.
