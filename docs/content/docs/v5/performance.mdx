These benchmarks run the same workflows on Workflow SDK v5.2.0 and v4.8.12, deployed to Vercel, and time the runtime at each point where a workflow waits on it: starting a run, moving from one step to the next, fanning out, resuming from a hook, and delivering a stream. [Methodology](#methodology) covers the setup and its limits.

## What each benchmark measures

Each benchmark starts a clock at one line of code and stops it at another. The work a step does is not counted, so the numbers measure the runtime itself.

- **Time to first step (TTFS)**: from your app calling [`start()`](/docs/api-reference/workflow-api/start) until the first line of the workflow's first step runs.
- **Step-to-step overhead (STSO)**: from one step finishing until the next step starts. This covers recording the step's result, resuming the workflow, and starting the next step.
- **Fan-out**: a workflow starts 64 steps at once with `Promise.all`. The first branch (TTFS) and the slowest branch to start, or time to last step (TTLS), are timed from `start()` until that branch's first line runs. The join is timed from the slowest branch finishing until the code after `Promise.all` runs.
- **Time to resume (TTR)**: a workflow waits on a [hook](/docs/foundations/hooks) for 1 s, then your app calls `resumeHook()`. TTR runs until the first line of the next step runs.
- **Chunk trip time (CTT)**: one step replays a recorded AI agent's token stream while another step reads it. CTT runs from the writer writing a chunk until the reader receives it.

Results are percentiles over every sample: half of the samples are at or below the p50 (the median), three quarters at or below the p75, and all but the slowest 1% at or below the p99.

## Steps and long runs

A five-step workflow measures how quickly a run reaches its first step and how much time the runtime adds between steps.

{/* generated:performance sequential */}

At p75, a five-step workflow reaches its first step in 172 ms on v5.2.0 and 418 ms on v4.8.12, and v5.2.0 adds 109 ms between steps where v4.8.12 adds 618 ms.

{/* end generated:performance sequential */}

A 1,020-step workflow measures whether that overhead grows with the run. On v4.8.12 it does, because each step resumes the workflow by replaying a longer [event log](/docs/how-it-works/event-sourcing). v5.2.0 avoids most of that replay: among other changes, it keeps the workflow's VM alive across steps that run in the same invocation and applies only the new events. See [What's new in v5](/docs/whats-new#faster-and-cheaper-runs).

{/* generated:performance long-run */}

Through a 1,020-step run, step-to-step overhead on v4.8.12 grows from 585 ms in steps 1 to 20 to 1,628 ms in steps 1,001 to 1,020, at p75. On v5.2.0 it is 110 ms and 70 ms. The median run takes 19.7 min on v4.8.12 and 3.0 min on v5.2.0.

{/* end generated:performance long-run */}

## Fan-out

A fan-out starts 64 one-step branches with `Promise.all`, then waits for all of them. The runtime queues the branches in batches, so they start close together and in no fixed order.

{/* generated:performance fanout */}

At p75, v5.2.0 starts the first of 64 branches in 360 ms against 478 ms on v4.8.12, then starts the slowest branch at 996 ms against 972 ms and joins in 448 ms against 464 ms. At p99, the slowest branch starts later on v5.2.0: 3,557 ms against 1,324 ms on v4.8.12, a gap that reproduced in a second sweep.

{/* end generated:performance fanout */}

Work to start a fan-out's queued branches sooner is in progress.

## Resuming a workflow

A workflow creates a [hook](/docs/foundations/hooks) and waits on it for 1 s, the way an agent waits for a person's approval, and then your app resumes it. Each run resumes five times.

{/* generated:performance resume */}

At p75, a workflow waiting on a hook resumes in 425 ms on v5.2.0 against 699 ms on v4.8.12, and in 820 ms against 1,128 ms at p99.

## Streams

One step replays a recorded AI agent's token stream in real time to a [namespaced stream](/docs/foundations/streaming#namespaced-streams) with [`getWritable()`](/docs/api-reference/workflow/get-writable), and a second step in the same run reads it with `getRun(runId).getReadable()`.

{/* generated:performance streams */}

The agent produced its 2,593-chunk response over 52.4 s. On v4.8.12 the writer step manages about 12 chunks per second, so the response takes 226.1 s to reach the reader. v5.2.0 writes streams over a WebSocket by default, keeps up at about 49 chunks per second, and delivers the response in 53.5 s.

At p75, a chunk reaches the reader in 102 ms on v5.2.0 against 172 ms on v4.8.12. v5.2.0 had a chunk delayed by over 1 s in 0 of 10 runs, against 6 of 10 on v4.8.12.

{/* end generated:performance streams */}

On the Vercel World, [`WORKFLOW_STREAMS_TRANSPORT`](/docs/configuration/worlds#workflow_streams_transport) sets the transport for stream writes.

## Methodology

- **Platform**: Both versions ran the same benchmark app on Vercel production in `iad1`, against the managed [Vercel World](/worlds/vercel), each on its own deployment. Before measuring, each deployment ran two short workflows and one fan-out that were not counted.
- **Workload**: Every step takes 1 KB of JSON in and out and does 100 ms of simulated work, which the overhead measurements exclude.
- **Runs**: {/* generated:performance runs */}Per version, 10 runs of the sequential workflow, 10 of the agent stream, 25 of the fan-out, 25 of the resume benchmark, and 5 of the 1,020-step run.{/* end generated:performance runs */}
- **Percentiles**: Each percentile pools every sample across a version's runs and uses the nearest-rank method.
- **Versions**: v4.8.12 is the published `workflow@4.8.12`. v5.2.0 was measured from `main` at `e6bd692`, whose code is the v5.2.0 release: the release commit only changes version numbers and changelogs.

### Limits

- The benchmarks ran in one region, against one World. Other regions, Worlds, and workloads will see different numbers.
- The benchmarks for both versions ran at the same time and shared the platform. {/* generated:performance notes */}The exception is the 1,020-step benchmark on v4, whose five runs ran earlier the same day, from 04:30 to 06:13 UTC, with the same configuration.{/* end generated:performance notes */}
- The explainers on this page are schematic: they show where each clock starts and stops, not real timings.
